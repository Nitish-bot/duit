# Approve Members individually and keep Pairing optional

Google Authentication identifies a person but grants no Duit data access by itself. Duit will use a server-controlled per-UID `memberApprovals/{uid}` registry: an administrator sets `approved == true` to admit each Member to an independently owned private workspace, any number of approved Members and independent Pairs may coexist, and an unapproved identity sees no workspace data. Pairing remains optional and limited to exactly two distinct approved Members with at most one active Pair per Member; every Task starts Private, and only an explicit Owner choice creates `Shared(pairId, shareGeneration)`. This replaces fixed-size backend admission and accepts manual approval work in exchange for simple early access control without hard-coding a single relationship into the authorization model.

This decision clarifies ADR-0001's “tiny two-person dataset” rationale as small independently owned Member workspaces, without changing its one-store decision. It supersedes ADR-0004's clauses that Duit consists of “exactly two independently owned Task systems connected by an exclusive Pair” and that “New Tasks default to Shared only while paired,” plus ADR-0005's fixed “two-UID allowlist” authorization clause. ADR-0004's immutable ownership, redacted Shared View, pair-bound Share Grant generations, and revocation limits remain in force; ADR-0005's narrow Functions/FCM boundary also remains in force.
